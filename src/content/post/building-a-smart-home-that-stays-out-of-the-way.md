The best home automation is the kind you never notice. Lights that are already on when you walk in. A house that's quiet because everything just works. The worst kind is the gadget that needs babysitting — the bulb that drops off the network, the routine that fires at 3am, the app that wants a login to turn off a lamp.

## A few principles I keep coming back to

- **Local-first.** If the internet goes down, the lights should still work. I lean toward setups that don't depend on someone else's cloud to function.
- **Privacy by default.** Automation shouldn't mean handing my living room to a third party. The fewer sensors phoning home, the better.
- **Boring is good.** Reliability beats features. I'd rather have five automations that always work than fifty that mostly do.

## Start small, then expand

It's tempting to automate everything at once. I've found it works better to nail one thing — say, lighting — and live with it for a while before adding the next layer. You learn what you actually want, and you avoid building a fragile tower of dependencies.

The funny thing is how much this overlaps with my day job: figure out the real problem, keep it reliable, and make sure it still works when you're not watching it.
